Composition inequality √τtotal ≤ Σ√τi verified across all 65 circuit configurations.

Experiment A: τ-chrono saves 29% total QPU shots. At nrep=8, saves 67% by avoiding unnecessary majority voting.

Experiment B: 3-qubit entangling mirror circuit. τ-chrono extends usable depth from 20 to 50 gates (2.5x). Two circuits saved that naive rejected.

April 2026 Update — Tuna-17 Hardware Push

A drill-down on three follow-up experiments. Results documented honestly, including what we did not achieve and why.

1. Full 24-pair Fanomaly sweep on Tuna-17

The v2 paper figure used three pairs (Δ Fanomaly = 0.22). A subsequent sweep of all 24 coupler-connected pairs shows the chip is far more non-uniform: spread = 0.825, mean = 0.55, std = 0.185. Best pair (q2-q5) F = 0.799 — about the same as Tuna-9's average pair, not better. Two pairs (q11-q14, q11-q13) are essentially dead.

Takeaway: on Tuna-17, "more qubits" buys you more reach (bigger molecules), not more accuracy per pair.

2. d=3 repetition code memory on Tuna-17 (Krinner-class reproduction)

Distance-3 rep code on chain q0–q1–q4–q2–q5, |0⟩L memory experiment with R ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5, 10} stabilizer rounds, 4096 shots each, decoded offline with stim + PyMatching. Result: 0 / 4096 logical errors at every R (plogical < 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ across 20480 logical measurements). Most physical errors concentrate in syndrome bits and are correctly classified as not affecting the logical qubit.

This reproduces Krinner et al. Nature 605 (2022) and prior QuTech rep-code work — it is not a discovery. The point is verifying that the τ-chrono toolkit drives Quantum Inspire's mid-circuit measurement workflow end-to-end.

Why It's Called τ — From Theory to Tool

1. Where τ comes from

τ-chrono is the hardware-realisation companion to the foundations paper "The Arrow of Time from Petz Recovery" (Huang 2025; Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.18897853, "Paper 1" in the Σ = 2 ln Q series).

Paper 1 establishes that the Petz recovery map (Petz 1986) is the unique Bayesian retrodiction functor on quantum channels (Parzygnat & Buscemi 2023), and that the temporal-asymmetry parameter

τ = 1 − F(ρ, R̃σ,N(N(ρ)))

is the precise quantitative measure of how far a quantum channel N is from being losslessly reversible. τ-chrono adopts this τ as its name and as the central observable: every fidelity, every gate, every circuit is tracked through the lens of "how much retrodiction has failed".

v1 was a noise-prediction tool: given a circuit, predict whether it will succeed. The headline result was 26.4% more accurate fidelity prediction than the standard independent-gate model on Tuna-9.

v2 is a noise-mitigation tool: take a real run that already happened and post-process it to recover the noise-free observable. The shift is from "predict whether the circuit works" to "fix the data so the circuit's answer is closer to what the noiseless circuit would have given". This is what enables the 3–10× error reduction on chemistry VQE.

3. How v2 actually works — anomalous weak values as a single-parameter probe

The mechanism behind v2 is the anomalous weak value (Aharonov–Vaidman 1988): a quantum measurement that uses both a past pre-selected state and a future post-selected state to extract a single-parameter signature of the channel acting between them. On a noiseless system, the anomalous weak value of the Π₀ projector is the famous negative-probability result that has been central to time-symmetric quantum mechanics since the 1980s.

τ-chrono v2 adopts this protocol as a noise probe. We measured it on four superconducting backends in April 2026 and found that one universal formula fits all of them within statistical noise:

<Π₀>w,observed = 1 − Fanomaly · <Π₁>w,theory(g)

The single parameter Fanomaly captures the entire process fidelity of the channel that connects the pre-selection to the post-selection — without needing a noise-model assumption per platform.

The anomalous-weak-value g-sweep used to extract Fanomaly. The pointer follows a single-parameter curve across the entire weak-coupling range; the slope is the channel's process fidelity. One probe circuit, one parameter, no noise model — that is the v2 calibration primitive.

Once Fanomaly is extracted, every Pauli expectation in a downstream chemistry VQE can be corrected as <P>corrected = (<P>observed − biasP) / FP. This is why v2 calibration costs 4–8× fewer circuits than Mitiq ZNE / PEC: instead of running the target circuit at multiple noise-amplification levels, we extract the channel fidelity from a separate single-parameter probe.

4. How tightly is each layer coupled to the original τ formula?

v1 and v2 are not equally tight to τ = 1 − F(ρ, R̃σ,N(N(ρ))). We state the connection strength honestly rather than gloss over it:

v1: σ-propagation trackerLiteral gate-by-gate Petz recovery on a Bayesian reference σ; the predicted F is the F in the formula.tight
v2: Fanomaly estimatorProcess-fidelity proxy from anomalous-weak-value g-sweep. Same limiting behaviour as F (= 1 iff channel reversible) but not literally the same number in the noisy regime. Trade-off: gives up literal Petz F to side-step the choice-of-σ problem on amp-damping channels (the IQM Garnet failure mode of v0.1).moderate
v2: per-Pauli (F, bias) calibrationLinear-correction layer per Pauli. Extension of v1's single-scalar F to a per-observable picture, but calibration values come from reference-state measurements, not Petz updates.loose

Plain reading. v1 is the τ formula in NISQ engineering form. v2's ABR layer is the τ framework's spirit (retrodiction-based mitigation) made measurable on hardware where literal Petz σ-propagation breaks down. The other v2 layers (symmetry verification, ZNE) are best-of-breed generic QEM stacked on top because they help — they are not τ-derived.

The honest v2 pitch. τ-chrono v2 = the τ-framework primitive (Fanomaly) + best generic QEM on top + a vendor-neutral cross-platform F estimator. The τ-specific layer wins 1.41× incremental precision over the Mitiq PS+ZNE baseline (verified A/B test, May 2026) and 4–8× cheaper calibration; the generic-QEM layers do most of the absolute error reduction.

Limitations

We believe in honest reporting. Here is what this approach cannot do.

  • Chemistry-vertical specialist, not universal NISQ tool. v2 ABR works on readout-dominated, shallow-ansatz workloads (chemistry VQE, ≤ 4 CZ depth). For deep optimisation (QAOA p ≥ 2, deep VQC) gain drops to < 10%; use Mitiq ZNE / PEC for those regimes.
  • Hardware coverage. Tested on Tuna-9 (full v2 sprint), Tuna-17 (24-pair F-shopping + d=3 rep code + H₂ accuracy push on q2-q5), IQM Garnet / Sirius / Emerald (single point each). IBM, Google, IonQ, AWS Braket: unverified.
  • Chemical accuracy on Tuna-class hardware is hardware-bounded. Even with the full PS + ABR + ZNE pipeline, H₂ on Tuna-17 q2-q5 bottoms out around 13 mHa — about 8× above the 1.6 mHa chemical accuracy target. Closing this gap requires IBM Heron / Google Willow class chips, not better algorithms.
  • Anomaly coherence is fragile. Tanomaly = 101 ns bare on Tuna-9, ~500 ns under X-Y-X-Y dynamical decoupling — about two orders of magnitude shorter than typical transmon T2*.
  • No fault-tolerant claims. All work is NISQ-era. Logical-qubit results require hardware that does not yet exist.
  • No retrocausal communication. The negative-probability anomalous weak value is a real, reproducible feature of post-selected weak measurements (Aharonov-Vaidman 1988); it is not a "see-into-the-future" channel. The protocol requires both a pre-selection and a post-selection, and standard interpretation is that the anomaly only appears in the conditioned ensemble retrieved after the post-selection event happens. Tanomaly is a hardware coherence number for this protocol, not a retrocausal signaling capacity.
  • v1 H₂ vs v2 H₂ are different experiments. The v1 H₂ figure (depth-4 viability claim) uses energies classically computed from noise-model density matrices propagated by the v1 tracker. The v2 NISQ Chemistry Vertical is full VQE on Tuna-9 / Tuna-17 hardware with 4096 shots and ABR v2 post-processing. They live in different layers of the stack — do not conflate.
  • Baseline strength is "naive multiplicative", not best-of-breed. The 26.4% / 48.3% v1 improvements are over independent-gate Bernoulli composition. We have not yet benchmarked against randomized / cycle benchmarking, gate-set tomography, Qiskit Aer calibrated noise model, or Mitiq ZNE/PEC head-to-head. That comparison is on the roadmap and is the right next benchmark sprint.
  • Cross-platform Fanomaly claim is "early empirical evidence", not "established universality". The 4-backend validation is a single-point per platform; multi-day robustness and Garnet full H₂ + ABR demonstration await monthly credit refresh.

How to Use

Add one line before you run your circuit. Works with Qiskit.

# Install
pip install tau-chrono

# With Qiskit circuit
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from tau_chrono.api import predict_circuit

qc = QuantumCircuit(3)
qc.h(0)
qc.cx(0, 1)
qc.cx(1, 2)

result = predict_circuit(qc)
print(result.should_run) # True
print(result.f_tauchrono) # 0.83
print(result.f_naive) # 0.82

Or without Qiskit:

from tau_chrono.api import predict_gates

result = predict_gates(["h", "cx", "cx", "h"] * 20)
print(result.should_run) # True or False
print(result.savings_pct) # 67.0%
